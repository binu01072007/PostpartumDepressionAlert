Pooled Prevalence of Postpartum Depression in India

EPDS Cut-off Validation (Levis et al., BMJ 2020)

Individual-participant meta-analysis: score cut-off ≥11 delivers the optimal balance of sensitivity (0.81) and specificity (0.88), while ≥13 provides high specificity for clinical intervention.

The Family Companion Guide

A practical, cultural handbook for husbands, partners, mothers-in-law, and relatives. Postnatal recovery requires community care, not isolated burden.

01

What to Watch For

Persistent crying spells, withdrawal from the newborn, expressing feelings of worthlessness, insomnia even when the infant sleeps, or intense agitation.

02

What NOT to Say

Avoid phrases like "Why are you sad when you have a healthy baby?" or "Every mother goes through this, toughen up." Hormonal and biochemical shifts require patience and support.

03

Practical Daily Actions

Take charge of night-time soothing shifts. Protect 4 hours of uninterrupted sleep for the mother. Ensure regular iron/calcium supplements and hydration.

04

Husbands can opt-in to receive a daily supportive prompt tailored to the baby's exact age in days.

Maternal Consent & Privacy Protocol

1. Scope of Automated Calls

Automated telephone check-ins occur daily across the first 14 days and at Week 6. Each call takes approximately 90 to 120 seconds, screening for physical recovery milestones and emotional wellness.

2. Escalation & Health Worker Notice

If physical warning signs (such as heavy bleeding, high fever, severe headache) or crisis self-harm ideation are reported, automated SMS notices will be dispatched to the mapped ASHA community worker and PHC.

3. Data Confidentiality & Number Masking

Phone numbers are masked in all logs (e.g., +91 98765 ••••10). Data is utilized strictly for triage escalation and is never sold or shared with commercial entities.

4. Voluntary Participation

The patient or family may opt out at any time by pressing 9 during a call or requesting the local ASHA worker to suspend the schedule.
